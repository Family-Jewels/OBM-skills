## Problem (pinpointed)

Depot-to-machine conversion, now chronic. From day 7 to day 19, stocked slots stayed at 4 to 8 of 72 while stock was bought in batches of 90 to 110 units, which arrive every 4 to 6 days. Between deliveries the machines empty; on day 19 the depot held 12 units against 200 units due on days 21 and 22 (€179 at cost), and 47% of worker time went unused with 64 empty slot positions. Frequency: every period since day 10 (A-001 saw the same pattern). Cost: revenue stays near €85 a day, and the gap to the exemplar pace widened by about €300 between days 10 and 19.

## Evidence

- **Stock held versus slots stocked.** Day 10: 82 units in the depot, 3 slots stocked. Day 18: 32 units, 6 slots. Day 19: 12 units, 4 slots (ground truth, product tables by slot).
- **Orders.** Seven orders paid days 1 to 18. Two are in transit (PO-1004 due day 21, PO-1007 due day 22). PO-1003 was paid on day 9 and delivered on day 11, and its stock was not loaded until day 16 to 17 (loaded day column).
- **Time.** Capacity used: day 10 38%, day 18 73%, day 19 53%. On a 480-minute day with 45-minute fills, several fills fit in the unused time on day 19 alone.
- **Opportunities (system counts, not my inference).** Day 19: 1 open (O2) against 3 expected from the spec's own conditions, since O1 needs unused time of at least 30% (47% here) with empty slots, and O3 needs the same plus cash above €500. Day 17: 3 open at 72% capacity used, when O1 and O3 need at least 30% unused and should have been closed. The opportunity logic and the capacity measure do not agree on these two days. The cause could be the time the system reads the clock, which I cannot see; it must be checked in the system.
- **Handoff, day 19.** "Open now" lists O2 only. Guidance given: G-003 (restock before buying more). The full A-001 summary, dated day 10, is included "for your information", with day-10 cash and slot figures.
- **Chart text.** `slots_selling` shows "IMPROVEMENT: 8 in a row above the centre". The measure counts sellers, and the spec defines it as stocked, priced slots with stock at day end, so the charted improvement is not the spec's capacity. The cash chart's "PIP = 1.8" is a level ratio (2,594 / 1,422); the spec's definition gives no value, since actual gain over the start is negative.

## What maintains it

- **Buying is rewarded, loading is not shown.** `unit_cost_paid_30d` falls when large orders are bought (the agent sees the measure as improving at 1.085 to 1.01 at its best), while the restraint on overbuying, `units_in_storage`, has limits too wide to signal. A large order looks good on the charts whether or not its stock reaches a machine.
- **Cash feedback penalises buying, not waiting.** `cash_change_per_day` read the day-9 order (€133) as a loss (−70 on day 10). Holding cash shows no loss; stocking slots pays off a period later, after sales. A shortcut that pays at once beats the path that pays late.
- **The advice points at the wrong fix.** G-003 says to restock empty slots before buying more, which is correct, but the handoff shows it alongside O2 only. G-002 (the O1 advice on filling slots first) is not shown, because O1 is not open on day 19.
- **Lumpy supply with no measure of it.** Supplier reliability is not measured (spec open question), so delivery timing shows only in units and slots, and nothing tells the agent that the depot will be empty until day 21.

## Cause

- **Information (primary).** (1) O1, the opportunity that names this exact situation, is not shown open on day 19 even though, on the spec's conditions, it should be. (2) The `slots_selling` chart reports an improvement that the stocked count does not show. (3) The handoff carries a day-10 audit as current and gives capacity as a percentage with no minutes-per-action, so the agent cannot easily see that a 45-minute fill fits in the time it left unused.
- **Incentives (secondary).** The measures reward large orders and show no sign for empty machines. Loading has no visible cost or reward until the next period's sales.
- **Resources.** Not the cause on the records: time was available on day 19, and the depot held stock to load on day 18. Supply timing is a real constraint from days 21 and 22 onward, but it does not explain days 10 to 18.
- **Knowledge or model tier.** Not supported by the records; the agent has run the core loop and sold to 67 units in a day.

Not verified: what the agent believed and planned. The record does not show whether it saw the O1 advice, whether it saw the charts (they sit in the same workspace tree, `swarm/`, and so are probably readable from it), or why it did not load the 12 depot units into empty slots on day 19.

## What the design would need to change (not written here)

1. Check the opportunity logic: O1 and O3 should show open on day 19 and were not shown on day 17. Confirm which time and capacity figures the system uses.
2. Make `slots_selling` count stocked, priced slots with stock at day end, as the spec defines it, and retire the current count. Re-chart it; a 4-of-72 level must read as low, not as improvement.
3. Chart the gap to the exemplar in euros against the pace line, not as a ratio against the start. Keep the PIP definition in the spec.
4. Stop passing full, stale audits in the handoff. Pass the latest pulse, dated, or a short current note.
5. Show, in the handoff, the minutes left in the day and the cost of the fills the empty slots need, so capacity is visible in the agent's own units.
6. Give `units_in_storage` limits that can signal, or show the stock-in-transit figure alongside it, so a large order does not look only like a lower unit cost.
7. Ask the worker what it saw on days 17 to 19 (charts, handoff, O1 advice), and read its notes and `log/work.jsonl` before the redesign, to confirm or drop the information cause.

Measures to move: `slots_selling` (stocked count), `revenue`, and the gap to the exemplar pace.
