# Plan stock orders around each supplier's lead time, across the run
Revises: G-001
Applies when: O1, O2, O3
What good looks like: A strong period notes how long each supplier's orders have taken from payment to delivery, and places the next order early enough that it lands before the depot runs out, so priced slots are not left empty waiting for stock. It reads the depot against the last few days of sales and the lead time together. For example, on day 68 the depot held 8 units, 25 priced slots were empty, and two orders paid on days 65 and 67 were not due until days 69 and 71.
Why: Of the 13 orders delivered by day 68, 12 arrived 2 to 5 days after payment, but PO-1022 took 12 days (paid day 52, delivered day 64; swarm/ground_truth/day-0068.md), and PO-1004 took 14 days (swarm/checks/C-005.md, from day-0037.md). The depot fell to 2 to 10 units on days 11 to 13 and on day 68 while priced slots were empty (metrics_system.md; ground_truth). G-001's example assumed stock on hand by day 2, which the first lead time ruled out; the lesson that still holds for the whole run is to plan orders against lead times.
Source: audit A-002
