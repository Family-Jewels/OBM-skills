# Opening days: plan around supplier lead times, then fill and learn
Revises: G-001
Applies when: always
What good looks like: In the first days, read each supplier's due day before expecting stock. An order paid on day 1 with a due day of 6 or 7 puts no units in the depot until then, so early days may have nothing to sell while the orders are on the way. A strong period uses those days to get the state of all six machines and the supplier catalogues, and to stock and price the slots that can sell as soon as delivered stock lands. After a few days of sales, it reads the sales report and lets it guide the next choices. For example, an opening where the first delivery lands on day 6 and every machine is stocked and priced within the day it arrives gives the sales report something to learn from by day 8.
Why: Revenue comes only from slots that are stocked and priced (slots_selling). In the records, PO-1002 paid on day 1 was due on day 6, and revenue stayed at zero from day 2 to day 7, so the earlier example (every machine stocked by day 2) could not be met. The reference operator reaches EUR 3,228 by day 30, so the first stock matters.
