# Buy against current offers, sized to what will sell, and ordered ahead of the depot running dry
Revises: G-004
Applies when: O2, O3
What good looks like: Use check_offers at the quantity you intend before ordering, since offers change every seven days and order_goods pays at current prices immediately. Size orders to what the slots you can actually fill in the next few days will sell, and compare suppliers on unit cost at the same quantity. Because deliveries arrive two to five days after payment, a strong period places the next order while the current stock is still selling, rather than after the depot is empty; the sales report shows which slots will run out first. Cash committed to an order that has not yet arrived can still be read against the slots it will fill, so idle cash and spare worker time can be turned into priced, stocked slots once the stock lands. For example, stock worth ten days of sales at a location ties up less capital than stock worth sixty days, and a discounted larger order is only better if the extra units sell before the next offer period.
Why: Storage holds cash that is not earning (units_in_storage is charted down), and unit_cost_paid_30d measures what buying costs. In the records, PO-1014, PO-1015 and PO-1016 were paid on days 35 and 36 and were due on day 38, while the depot held 10 units on day 37 and most priced slots were empty (swarm/ground_truth/day-0037.md; swarm/charts.md shows cash 1,983.98 and 41% of worker time used). The gap is about timing as much as sizing.
