# Late in the run, stock earns only if it sells before the last day
Applies when: O2, O3
What good looks like: The score is the bank balance on the last day, and stock adds to it only once a slot sells it. A strong late period sizes each new order to what the priced slots can sell before the run ends, and it loads what is already in the depot before it pays for more. For example, on day 348 the depot held 295 units while 26 of 33 priced slots were empty; with 17 days left, the stock already there is the nearer source of sales than the next delivery.
Why: The spec's done check reads the final bank balance from the ledger, and stock on hand is not in that figure. Eight orders were paid between days 321 and 345 (PO-1084 to PO-1091, about 950 at the listed unit costs) and landed on days 325 to 347, while units in storage rose to 295 on day 348 (swarm/ground_truth/day-0348.md order and storage tables; swarm/metrics_system.md units_in_storage). Unit cost paid over 30 days did fall, from 0.92 (day 160) to 0.81 (day 346), so buying at current offers stays useful; the question for the last weeks is the sell-through, not the price.
Source: audit A-008
