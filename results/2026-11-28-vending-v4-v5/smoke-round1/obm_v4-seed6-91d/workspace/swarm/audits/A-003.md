2. **Fix, human and system: the Pursuit split is divided by logged minutes, not by 480, so the restocking alarm is false.** spec.md v2 and aims.md define a share as minutes over 480. The Pursuit and Facts shares do not. Recomputed from actions.jsonl periods 71 to 77 (per-call costs from the Facts table): restock 1,080 min, all logged calls 1,415 min. 1,080 / 1,415 = 76%, which is the charted 75%. On the 480 base: restock 32%, sourcing 2% (75 min: 2 check_offers, 1 order_goods), pricing 1% (30 min), reading 6%, and about 58% of the day unused. M-002 (day 46) said capacity = per-call cost x calls / 480, but the Pursuit row never adopted it. P-009, P-010, P-011, D-009 and S-004 all argued restocking at 70 to 73% against 35%. The fault that holds is sourcing and pricing starved while time sits idle, not restocking in excess. Fix: the system divides Pursuit by 480 (REQ-019). Until then, no control or aim should act on the 75% figure.

3. **Fix, designer: the worker's brief has not changed since day 0, the point-11 aims never reached the worker, and the designer's open requests are weeks old.** rules/operator.md is identical to history/operator.v1.md. Its Done line still counts "restock or pricing rather than reading" as success, and rule 12 restocks unconditionally. No AS-004 exists. aims.md and instructions.md still carry AS-003 (as of day 60) with 35% restocking. Fix: rewrite the brief, issue AS-004 for point 12, and answer REQ-014.

4. **Fix, controls: the one control in force is not over-controlling, but its effect cannot be shown, and the 35% cap ask is moot.** The control set is one line: read the depot before each restock. It costs one 25-minute read per restock loop, which the aims already require, and it adds no cap. Total review cost is $0.76 (charts System health). The effect cannot be judged, because the action log records only tool, period and clock, not what the read showed (C-007). Review point 11 is due now.
