Request handled: REQ-006 (from D-001, asked 17 times, key capacity-split-drift). It asks for two things: gate restock on the same-period depot read, and hold the restocking share to the 35% in the Pursuit table (70% at the last point).

## What I checked
- Charts (Pursuit, Facts, Resources): restocking 70% of capacity at point 10 against a 35% plan. Worker time median 255 of 480 min used, so capacity is idle, not binding. The Facts table gives restock_machine 45 min a call and get_storage_inventory 25 min.
- Controls in force: the depot-read gate (line 4), unchanged since day 56 (C-006). Review point 10 is due now (days 64-70).
- Ground truth, actions.jsonl periods 57-67 (lines 440-519): each period is one get_storage_inventory read, then 3 to 5 restock_machine calls. The log records tool, period and clock only; it does not record what a read showed. So from ground truth I can confirm a read precedes each restock loop, but I cannot confirm whether a restock followed a read showing stock.
- Diagnosis D-007 (confirmed) and C-006 audit: the maintaining cause is the operator brief, which still restocks unconditionally. The in-stock rule and the split in aims.md (AS-003) have not reached the brief (REQ-009, open with designer).

## Decisions
1. Depot-read gate: kept. It is still live (restocking is 70% of capacity, and restocks run after reads each period), and it costs one 25-minute read per restock loop, a small share. It is the smallest control that closes the empty-shelf fault. Review point moved from 10 to 11 (the default control gap).
2. 35% restocking hold: declined. The 35% is the aim split in aims.md, which belongs to the improvement branch. A hard cap would stop restocking, which is core work (the shelves must be filled for any sale), and C-001 and C-006 already declined this. The split is not a missing limit; the fix is in the brief, not in a second control.
3. No new control added. A per-call or per-share check would add cost to the same loop without a new risk.
