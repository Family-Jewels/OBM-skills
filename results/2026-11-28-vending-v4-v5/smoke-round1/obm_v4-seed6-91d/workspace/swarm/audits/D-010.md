# D-010: capacity-split-drift (day 73 of 91)

**Pinpoint:** restock_machine takes 71% of worker minutes in the last point against a 35% plan (45 min a call, 250 calls). Sourcing is 13% against 35%; pricing 2% against 10%. Restock loops run 3 to 5 calls after one depot read, including reads showing no stock (P-011, periods 65-69). Moved: revenue and units celerate the wrong way (/1.15, /1.11), cash_gain_7 is beyond its lower limit, slots_selling is flat, unit_cost_paid_30d is rising. Median worker time used is 240 of 480 min, so time is not the binding limit: minutes go unused while sourcing is short.

**Maintained by:** restock is the only action that fills slots in the same period, so it pays at once; sourcing and pricing pay late or unseen (D-008). The depot-read gate limits when a restock may happen, not how much time it takes, and the control states it sets no share, so nothing bounds the drift. No per-product read result is recorded, so a restock of an empty shelf has no consequence.

**Cause, environment first:**
1. Information: the split and the depot rule are in aims and controls, but whether a product had stock at restock is not measured (REQ-013), so the gate cannot be checked.
2. Resources: the depot has been empty since day 23 (D-007). Sourcing is the only refill path and orders run about once a point against a target of 3. Unverified: whether check_offers quotes met the margin rule, which would explain the low order count.
3. Incentives: restock pays at once and nothing caps its minutes.
4. Worker: it follows the immediate payoff. Its own log is empty (0 bytes), so its reasoning cannot be checked.

**Would change:** a cap on restock minutes per point, or a binding share (controls). Evidence needed to settle cause 2: the check_offers results with their margins.
