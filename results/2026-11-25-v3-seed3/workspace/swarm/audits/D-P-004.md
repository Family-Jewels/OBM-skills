Slots_selling, revenue, units and revenue_per_slot are all flat (celeration x1.02 to x1.18, provisional). Their latest values sit well under their centres (units 21 vs 60; revenue 45 vs 126; slots 14 vs 17). Meanwhile 92% of capital (2,971 of 3,246) and 56 of 72 slots sit idle, and slots go empty for days waiting on deliveries. Unit cost is rising (x1.03, wrong way).

- aims.md (v2, day 23) expired at day 28. No point-5 aim and no S-002 are on record; the Strategist is due at point 6.

**What maintains it.**
- The spec's budgets cap weekly purchases at "what the next week's refills can carry". That ties capital to a worker-time constraint the records do not support, so idle cash stays idle.
- The check_offers rule chooses by unit cost. Cheap long-lead suppliers win (bakker crisps at 1.17 with 10–21 days, "too slow for now"). Each stockout costs sales at the moment of demand, but no measure charges it. Unit cost is scored; cover is not.
- The operator cut its reads for token budget (progress.md, D33). That reduced the daily stock and clock reads the spec names as ground truth, which is why the worker-time record is in doubt.

**Cause.**
- *Information:* the spec names depot cover (2a) as the supply link to push, but charts.md carries no depot-cover row and aims.md sets no cover aim. The constraint named in S-001 (worker time) is not supported by the system's own clock readings.
- *Resources:* idle capital and idle slots exist while slots_selling is short; the binding link is supply, not labour. Minutes by tool are still unpublished.
- *Incentives:* the unit-cost check rewards long-lead suppliers; the purchase cap and the reduced reads reward doing less.

**What the design would need to change.** Measure depot cover and lead time with a chart and an aim. Make unit cost a guardrail, not a driver. Remove or rewrite the refill-linked purchase cap. Publish the daily clock and stock readings regardless of token budget. The Strategist should re-name the constraint at point 6 from those readings.
