## Direction
- The driver tree still points at the right top measure (final cash on day 91), and the goal is unchanged.
- The top measure and the goal are right, but the design has two faults that make the current aims hard to read and hard to meet:
  1. The Pursuit table compares a plan on the 480-minute day with actuals on minutes used. Restock reads 63% of used minutes, which is about 176 of 480 minutes (37%), so restock is on plan. Price setting reads 12% of used minutes, about 34 of 480 minutes (7%), against a 20% plan. The price shortfall is real; the restock "overrun" is mostly a denominator artefact (D-008 said the same).

The operator brief's first rule also says "fill before any other task", which the aims' order of the day (price first) contradicts. So the worker met conflicting instructions about the day's first action.

## Diagnosis of M-001's missed prediction (REQ-020)
- M-001 predicted supplier purchasing at 10% of the day by day 18. The Pursuit table now reads 5% of used minutes (about 3% of the day), and the charts show it at 2% by day 18.
- The cause is in the cost of the order cycle, which M-001 did not count. Each order needs a check_offers call (25 min) and an order_goods call (25 min), about 50 minutes per order, and the T-0002 quotes expire after 7 days, so each order needs a fresh quote (T-0002.md line 3). Orders ran on days 43, 46 and 50, about every 3–4 days, not weekly, so the plan's 10% was a weekly-cycle figure applied to every day.
- Decision: keep M-001's board-status rule. It costs no worker minutes and is not a purchasing driver, so reverting it would not change purchasing. The purchasing share in the aims stays at 10% as a ceiling on minutes, not a target.
