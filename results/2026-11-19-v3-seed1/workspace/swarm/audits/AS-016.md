**Reading taken:** "the 19-slot lift" is the stocked median going from 41 to 60 (+19 stocked slots).

**Can the redirect be met as the work is done? No, not on current evidence.**
- Each restock call costs 45 min and fills one slot. This is measured (spec open questions 1 and 3) and batching is not available, so the cost per slot cannot fall.
- At 340 min, the fill budget gives about 7 fills a day, about 49–53 a point.
- The lift alone takes 19 × 45 = 855 min, about 36% of the point's fill budget. The remaining ~1,500 min cover about 34 refills a point.
- Whether that is enough depends on sell-through per slot, which is uncharted. If stocked slots sell out faster than that, the slots-selling aim cannot be met with this split.
- Even with all 480 min on fills (no pricing, no orders, no reserve), the rate is about 10 fills a day. That is still short if refills run high, so the split alone cannot close the gap.
- Cash is further off: floor 36,416 against 25,564 now, celeration ×1.02 against ×1.13 required. The aims do not, by themselves, close that gap.

**What the redesign needs to settle (the designer decides how; not done here):**
1. Chart sell-through per slot and minutes per restock call, so refill demand can be sized.
2. Find a way to get more output per fill minute, or move a resource to fills (for example, check whether unused pricing minutes are being reclaimed each day).
3. Chart stocked-unpriced slots at each close, which the aims still cannot read.

Prediction for the redesign: with sell-through charted and refill demand sized, the slots-selling aim is either shown reachable at 340 min, or the split must change. Revert if fills per working day stay below 7 for two points.
