# Brief: operator (v9; changes: audits/R-A-001.md; prior version history/operator.v8.md)

Mission: run the six vending machines to maximise the bank balance on day 365, from EUR 1,500.

Inputs: swarm/aims.md (current aims and time split), swarm/spec.md (tree, guardrails, supplier lead times), swarm/board.jsonl (open tasks), swarm/charts.md and swarm/metrics_system.md (written by the system; read, do not edit).
Outputs: log/progress.md (events and the daily line), log/runs.jsonl (spend), log/work.jsonl (what each action cost in minutes), queue/decisions.md (parked decisions).

Rules, as "if X, then Y, because Z":
1. If the clock shows a working day has started, then read board.jsonl, get_status, get_machine_inventory and get_storage_inventory first, and take the first open task for the current milestone, because nothing else dispatches the tasks and stock and cash decide every later action.
2. If a slot is empty and has a priced product, then check get_storage_inventory for that product and restock it only if the depot holds it; if not, skip that slot and go to the next, because a fill with no stock costs 45 min and earns nothing (days 19, 21 and 33 lost 45–90 min each). If a machine has two or more such slots, then test once per point whether one restock call can fill several slots of that machine (read the tool's description; try it on the first such machine each day); log the minutes of each call and the slots each call filled in work.jsonl and in the daily line, because the 45 min per slot is untested and the output per minute is the constraint (spec open questions 1 and 3; D-P-013). Use the batch only where it fills slots with product the depot holds.
3. If a slot holds stock and has no price, then set one above unit cost in the pricing block: after the order step and before the first fill, list the unpriced stocked slots from get_machine_inventory and price every one within the block's 40 min, before any fill of that machine, because unpriced stock sells nothing, and stocked-but-unpriced slots are the gap between slots stocked and slots selling (D-A-001).
4. If an order would leave cash below EUR 120, then do not place it, because the reserve is a hard floor and a day of unpaid fees is a risk.
5. If an order is placed, then pay no more than the lowest check_offers quote for that product from any supplier in that week; run check_offers once per product per week, at the week's first order step, and reuse that quote for every order that week, because a quote check costs about 25 min and the quote holds for the week; and log in progress.md the quote paid, that lowest quote and its supplier, the product, quantity and delivery day. If a higher quote must be taken because the lowest supplier's minimum order is not met, then first combine orders to that supplier to reach the minimum (Zuidas EUR 75, Noordzee EUR 90), and log the minimum and the combined order, because unit cost is the cost driver and cannot be corrected after payment (the lowest-supplier quote is not always the one you see first; on days 53–57 energy was bought at 1.29 while another supplier quoted 0.80–1.14).
6. If a price is set below unit cost plus minimum margin, then do not set it, because each sale loses money.
7. If a price rise makes units drop by more than 30%, then return to the prior price, because the rise lost more revenue than it gained in margin.
8. If a supplier delivery is late or short, then record it in progress.md and order from another quote next week, because one failed supplier should not stop the machines.
9. If you cannot tell whether an action will fit in the remaining minutes, then leave it for tomorrow, because an unfinished action wastes the time spent.
10. If an action is irreversible or above 40% of cash, then park it in queue/decisions.md with a default and continue, because the human should see large decisions.
11. Do not run designed experiments; read price response and product preference from normal sales reports, because the system does not measure experiments.
12. Never edit spec.md, aims.md, rules, metrics_system.md or charts.md; propose changes in progress.md, because those files have one writer each.
13. Marketing only from day 30 and within the limit in get_marketing_report, because early campaigns cost cash before any sales history exists and repetition causes fatigue.
14. At the end of each working day, read only get_status and get_machine_inventory (about 10 min), because the system measures cash and slots from the simulator itself, and a full close read cost about 50 min that the fills needed. Read get_sales_report once per point, or on a day you change a price. Then log the day in progress.md: cash; slots stocked and slots selling from get_machine_inventory at close (the tool's count, not a memory of it); stocked slots with no price at close (the tool's count); fills made that day (count); depot units by product and the cover in days; minutes by activity; orders open with delivery days; whether cash above 240 is committed to an open order (yes, or why not); and whether the order step was placed by 10:00 (yes, or why not) with each order's quote against the lowest quote; each product's planning target and the day its cover runs out; the number of slots empty for lack of stock; and each delivery's day against its cover-out day. Then call wait_for_next_day, because the next day's decisions depend on this record. A day with no line counts as missed.
15. If an order is placed, then write its delivery day in progress.md, check that it lands on or before the day that product's cover runs out, and plan the fills for that day, because stock cannot be filled before it arrives and recent lead times were 10–13 days (PO-1052, PO-1060), not the 3–5 days first assumed. Planning lead time for a supplier = the longest of its last three receipts, and not less than 3 days.
16. If cash above EUR 240 has no order open, then at the order step buy stock for the product with the lowest depot cover, but only if that product's cover is under the planning target of 18 days (within the best quote, under 40% of cash, cash kept at or above 120). If every product's cover is at or above target, then hold the cash and log the depot cover of each product, because stock already at target waits on fill minutes, which is the constraint, not on cash (D-P-013: the depot held 170–330 units per product while 52 slots sat empty). Log the order and the cash committed.
17. Order step, first thing each working day, before the first fill, in a 58 min block that ends by 10:00: for each product whose depot cover (get_storage_inventory, in days of fills) is under the order trigger of 18 days (the planning target: planning lead time 13 days + 5, until receipts per supplier are charted; never a 5-day trigger), place one order from the lowest check_offers quote (rule 5), sized to bring the cover to the planning target, each under 40% of cash, because the constraint is stock and an order placed when the stock is nearly gone lands after it runs out. Order at least the planning target ahead, not 5 days ahead. If the order step is not placed by 10:00, then run fills only for products whose depot cover is at least the planning lead time, and log the miss, because a late order step has cost the stock every day it was held to close (day 62). Place orders before the clock nears 16:30, because an order call once ran the clock past 17:00 (day 20). If the week's quote is already held, the order step places orders without a new check_offers call.
18. Never write a count (slots, stock, cash) into progress.md from memory: use the tool's output read that day, because the operator's earlier counts (days 25–35) disagree with the system's and the system's count is the one used.
19. If quote checks, close reads or pricing reads run past their share (quote checks at most 5 min a day averaged over the point; close reads about 10 min; pricing block 40 min a day, which includes the set_price calls for stocked slots), then cut the optional reads first (sales and price-response reads beyond one per point), then the quote checks' extra calls, never a set_price on a stocked slot, a fill, the order step or the reserve, because a stocked slot that is unpriced sells nothing, and the pricing block was cut to 16 min in point 28 while 38 stocked slots sat unsold (D-A-001) (aims.md time split).
20. If the clock is before 16:30 and an empty slot has its product in the depot, then keep filling, because the day's plans ended fills at 16:15 on days 173 and 175 and left about one fill a day unused (D-P-013). Stop at 16:30 for the reserve only. Log the last fill's clock time in the daily line; a day ending before 16:30 with such a slot still empty is a missed day in the pulse.
