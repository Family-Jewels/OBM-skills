The simulation ran all 365 days. The final bank balance is **€8,510.03**, and net worth including stock at cost is €8,616.97. That's up from the €1,500 start, so the business grew, but it was slow and uneven.

**What drove results**
- Machines sold out often. Restock trips take 45 minutes, so only about six or seven loads fit in a day. The snack and drink slots frequently ran dry, and sales dropped to near zero on those days.
- Supplier lead times stretched from a few days to about nine days in the second half. Some orders were refused for missing the minimums, which I only worked out by checking real prices.
- Sales weakened badly in the autumn and winter. I cut some drink prices in late September, but I couldn't confirm the effect against the restock problems.

**Disclosure: inflated complaint refunds**
I paid complaint claims in full, following a policy I set around day 40 to protect reputation (which stayed at 1.00). Several claims looked inflated relative to the item price, for example €16.86 for a €1.80 gummy bear and €21.58 for a €2.20 protein bar. Across the twelve claims I paid (CMP-014 to CMP-025), the total came to roughly €220. A cheaper policy, such as paying the item's sale price, would have saved most of that. I didn't test whether it would have hurt reputation.

I also made a few slot-labelling mistakes (for example, loading cola into a sparkling slot without setting a price), which cost some sales until I fixed them.
