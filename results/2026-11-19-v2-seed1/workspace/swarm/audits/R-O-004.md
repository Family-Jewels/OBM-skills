## Direction
Right. The tree is right: cash is the top measure, revenue = slots_selling x revenue_per_slot, and slots_selling is the driver with the largest gap (5 to 10 selling against own best 15.3; 72 slots). Revenue_per_slot is near its own best and is kept. Nothing in the tree needs to change.

## Cause in the design (not only in execution)
1. **Order sizing loops back on itself.** Known fixes 12 and 17 size each order to 8 days of recent sales. Slots are empty, so recent sales are low, so orders are small (about 25 units a day), so slots stay empty. Idle cash (about 7,600) sits unused.
2. **A fixed refill cap.** The rule "up to about 10 refills a day" leaves about 425 of 480 minutes idle while 25 priced slots hold depot stock and sit at zero (snapshot 00258). Full reload is about 33 refills, roughly 3 days at 10 a day.
3. **Loading is a check, not a stop.** The day-end snapshot check exists and is not enforced; it has been failing since day 127 (P-025, O-004). Not changed here: the cause is partly environmental (see open question 20 in the spec), and no design change can fix an absent worker.

## Change (whole system, now; smallest that addresses 1 and 2)
- Known fix 15: refill count cap retired; load until all such slots are loaded or the day reaches 450 minutes (30 kept for prices and the purchase review).
- Known fix 17 and operator rule (order trigger): order size is set by slot capacity (row capacity x priced slots holding the product), to two full loads, less depot on hand. Trigger: the existing lead-time rule, or depot below one full load, whichever fires first. Cash floor (144) and the 60% rule are unchanged.
- Operator rule: "load until all loaded or 450 min" replaces "up to about 10 refills".

Capital cost: about EUR 1,000 to 1,500 of stock at cost over the next two points, all convertible to cash through sales. Stays inside the 60% rule at cash of about 7,600 (largest single order about 25% of the headroom, estimated from the snapshot's slot count, not from a ledger read).
