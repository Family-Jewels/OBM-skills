## Criterion 1: the change is on every unit of the slice. **Unmet.**

Day 32 had all 12 AI-HOUSE slots with product assigned and quantity 0 (drinks A1–A3 and snack A1–A3, C1 assigned; the rest unassigned). Day 51 shows loads on AI-HOUSE, so the rule was partly applied. But the rule was not met for units still in depot:

| Depot stock (day 51 storage table) | Landed | Unit cost | Unit margin at AI-HOUSE price | Empty AI-HOUSE slot with that product (day 51) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| oat-latte-can, 12 | day 45 | 1.57 | 2.50 − 1.57 = 0.93 | AI-HOUSE:DRINKS:A3 (qty 0) |
| choco-bar-100g, 6 | day 45 | 0.71 | 2.00 − 0.71 = 1.29 | AI-HOUSE:SNACK:A3 (qty 0) |
| dropjes-100g, 5 | day 45 | 0.59 | 1.80 − 0.59 = 1.21 | AI-HOUSE:SNACK:B1 (qty 0) |
| sparkling-water-500ml, 10 | day 45 | 0.51 | 1.50 − 0.51 = 0.99 | none empty (AI-HOUSE:DRINKS:B2 holds 5, loaded day 48) |

All four lines have positive unit margin. The first three have an empty AI-HOUSE slot already assigned to the same product. These units landed on day 45 and were still unloaded on day 51, six days later, against a rule of "within 24 hours of landing". This is the mismatch. The rule as written was not carried out on every unit of the slice.

The day-48 operator note says sparkling water was loaded into DRINKS:B2 and nut mix into SNACK:A2. The day-51 snapshot is consistent with that load, but it does not clear the 10 sparkling units still in depot, and it does not make the oat, choco and dropjes loads on time. The note is a claim; it was not used as evidence.

Daily snapshots cannot show loads of units that landed and sold within a day. So the check here rests on the units still in depot, which is enough to fail the criterion.

AI-LOUNGE and MAIN-LOUNGE changed between day 32 and day 51 (for example AI-LOUNGE:SNACK:A1 choco loaded day 46; MAIN-LOUNGE:SNACK:A2 choco loaded day 46; MAIN-LOUNGE:DRINKS:A1 sparkling loaded day 50). These are ordinary restocking after the day-45 delivery, which the Change line allows on control. Daily snapshots cannot fully separate rule-driven control loads from ordinary restocks, so this is a "no contradicting evidence" result, not proof of absence.
