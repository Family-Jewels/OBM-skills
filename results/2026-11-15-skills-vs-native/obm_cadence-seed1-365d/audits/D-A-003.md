## Pinpointed problem

Since day 140, drinks and snack slots sit empty for days at a time. Slots selling average about 11 of 72 (≈15%), not the ≥60% M3 needs. Each restock trip fills one slot, and trips run 8–9 per day at 45 minutes each. The operator's own trip logs for days 143–165 show one slot loaded per trip (for example day 165: 8 trips, 9 entries, one product per trip). Some days have no trips at all ("not worth a trip", days 142 and 147), because depot stock is small.

## What maintains it

- The R-P-020 intervention changed the load quantity (drinks loaded to capacity, 10/10). It did not change how many slots a trip covers. Trip count and slots per trip stayed flat.
- Before day 140 the log shows the same pattern: day 72 (PO-1027 landing) gave 9 trips for about 9 slots. So loads per trip did not rise after R-P-020. A-003's test (a) answers no.
- Stock-outs follow from restock timing. Choco, nuts and stroopwafel were out days 146–148; crisps out at day 150. An empty slot costs sales every day, while skipping a small trip costs nothing visible to the operator.

## Cause (environment, not performance)

- **Information:** the brief does not say how many slots one restock visit may cover, and the spec's trip measure is not charted, so the operator cannot see a slots-per-trip target. Order-by-cover uses upper lead times (noordzee 5, zuidas 11) but deliveries have landed one day late, which the brief does not model.
- **Resources:** the trip budget (8–9 visits of 45 minutes) is the binding constraint. R-P-020 assumed loads per trip would rise; the records show they did not.
- **Incentives:** the ranking rule (expected sales) gives no weight to idle slots on days with no trips.

Zero-stock days explain part of the shortfall, but not all of it: the fall began on day 140, before the late-stock days.

## Design would need to change

Make slots-per-trip and trips-per-day visible as charted driver measures (the tree's trip driver is still uncharted), and state the visit capacity in the brief. Move the cover trigger to the actual landing lag. Measure against slots_selling (M3's ≥60% of capacity).

## Evidence still needed

- Ledger split of cash_gain_7, to check how much of the drop is revenue versus COGS or write-offs.
- Checker sample of the trip logs against system stock readings.
- Day-label offset (A-002) is still open; it does not change the direction of the slots shift but affects day-level matching.
