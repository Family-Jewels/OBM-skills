## What maintains it

- The buy calendar was weekday and offer-validity driven ("next buy Mon 30 Mar", "offers valid to day 84"), not cover-driven. No cover-days figure appears in `log/progress.md` after day 75, though brief v3 requires one before each order.
- Orders for the longest-lead supplier were placed as the stock ran out, so each one landed after the gap had opened (PO-1031 placed day 79, lands day 90; PO-1033 placed day 81, lands day 85).
- Trips and loads are the stated constraint (day 86: 7–9 trips a day). Drink slots were loaded at 10 units; snack slots at up to 18.

## Cause

- **Information (primary):** Brief v3 says supplier lead time is 2–4 days. The ledger contradicts this. Noordzee lands in 2–6 days. Zuidas lands in 4–11 days (PO-1021: 11; PO-1031: 11). Bakker-en-zoon landed in 6 and 15 days. Orders placed on the 2–4 day figure land too late for the stock they are meant to cover. The brief also gives no cover-days formula the operator can apply daily.
- **Resources (secondary):** Trip capacity is the binding constraint on days with stock on hand, and drink loads of 10 units per slot may not fill the trip (slot capacity is not in the records; see open evidence).
- **Incentives:** No sign of a reward for the wrong behaviour. The operator acted on pivots P-012 and P-013 within the day. Not the cause.
- **Environment, not performance:** The ledger split of `cash_gain_7` and `log/runs.jsonl` are still absent after P-012 and P-013. These are orchestrator and system items. They block the budget check but did not cause the stock-out.
- **Knowledge / model tier:** Not indicated.
