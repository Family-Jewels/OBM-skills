## Problem (pinpointed)

Product lines sit at zero depot stock while their slots are empty, for several days at a time, and then reload late. Observed: all three machines empty on days 84–86; MAIN empty on day 91; crisps, stroopwafel, nuts and choco at zero for days 93–97; choco, stroopwafel and crisps empty at all three machines on day 104; sparkling A2 empty at all three on days 106–109. Each stock-out follows a cover-rule date that passed without an order.

## Evidence

- Progress days 99, 100 and 103: crisps and choco cover fell below the order-by date (order-by passed), and the operator wrote "order on the next zuidas buy" and deferred. Crisps was empty from day 101 to 104. Choco was empty from day 104 to 106.
- Lead times are 3–11 days (zuidas), so a deferral costs the stock-out days that follow, not the day it was deferred.
- Operator days 86 and 92: "restock trips (7–8 per day) are the constraint". Trips per day is not on any chart or in the spec tree.
- Operator day 93 says the day-91 trough "meets the R-P-014 prediction". The system's day 91 units is 6, and the prediction is ≥15. The operator's labels are one day ahead: "sales day N" is system day N+1 (checked against metrics rows 92, 93 and 100).
- `metrics_system.md` has no rows for days 68, 80, 98 or 101.

## What maintains it

- Deferral pays at once: it saves an order and a supplier-timing decision today. The stock-out cost arrives 3–11 days later, after the buy.
- Weekly buy cadence (spec, cadence table) competes with the cover rule (brief). The operator often accepts the weekly slot as the buy day, against "buy by cover, not by weekday."
- R-A-001 moved slots to "check only" and the brief says "do not fill a slot only to raise slots_selling". M3's 60% slot aim is still in spec v2, so the aim and the rule conflict. Nothing is set that makes a stocked, selling slot count, so the operator has no reason to keep slots full.

## Cause

- **Information (primary).** The rule for when to buy is clear, but the spec's weekly buy cadence contradicts it. The day labels in the operator's records are offset from the system's, so the R-P-014 verdict was judged on the wrong day. M3's slot aim conflicts with the brief's slot rule.
- **Resources (secondary).** Restock trips are the binding constraint (7–9 a day against 6 machines × 12 slots). The design measures neither trips nor operator time per restock. Depot counts are estimated, not read, which caused load failures (days 89, 96).
- **Incentives.** Deferring a buy is rewarded at once and punished 3–11 days later. Empty slots that the operator is not pushed to fill cost nothing in the operator's measures.

## What the design would need to change (not written here)

- Measure trips per day and operator minutes per restock, and make restock timing depend on them.
- Resolve the weekly-buy cadence against the cover rule; decide which governs and make the record say so.
- Resolve M3's slot aim against the slot rule, and move the trigger to system days.
- Reconcile the PIP basis and the cash_gain_7 window, and add the missing system rows (68, 80, 98, 101).
- Measure zero-stock days per product directly from the system, so the deferral cost is visible on the day it happens.
