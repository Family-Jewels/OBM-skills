## 2. Pinpoint

1. The cash measure is a ledger cash change (spec gap). Stock purchases count as cash out in the week bought. Fuller stock after R-A-003 raises purchases, so part of the signal may be inventory build, not lost margin. Not yet separable.
2. Restock visits on days 180–188 are logged as one slot per trip (e.g. day 187: 8 loads, 8 trips). The v6 brief (rule 19) requires several slots per visit and, if not possible, a same-day note. No note exists.
3. Several products have zero depot stock on the same days (day 187: crisps, nuts, stroopwafel, oat). Empty slots cannot be filled whatever the visit rule.
4. Crisps and nuts ordered day 188 with a 10-day lead; the day-186 plan said order day 187.

## 3. What maintains it

- One-slot-per-trip costs nothing visible. Whether a visit can take more than one slot is untested: the tool's restock signature is not in the records, and the system's per-visit restock events, owed since R-P-020, are not charted.
- Trip counts are operator self-report and internally inconsistent (day 187: "7 trips" then "8 loads for 8 trips"; day 184: 8 loads on 7 trips).
- Depot-zero days are reinforced by the reward for buying late: a cheaper or delayed order costs nothing that day.

## 4. Cause, environment first

- **Information:** the cash measure does not separate purchases from margin, so the redesign cannot be judged on it. The per-visit slot count is not recorded by the system.
- **Resources:** the tool's per-visit capacity is unknown. Depot stock is short on several products.
- **Incentives:** a visit that fills one slot is not penalised; the cover rule is not enforced at the order date.
- Model tier is not the first suspect.

## 5. What the design needs (not written here)
