### 3. Direction

- **The tree adds up.** Revenue = slots × revenue per slot holds on the metrics rows (day 200: 190.0 = 19 × 10.0). Slots is the driver that moves.
- **The trip driver the tree relies on has a ceiling the redesigns did not model.** The operator's day-195 and day-200 entries (`log/progress.md`) say each restock call fills one slot, and that this has held on every call since day 1. Trips are 45 minutes in an 8-hour day, so at most about 8 slot refills a day. R-P-020 assumed fuller loads per visit, and R-A-003 assumed "2 or more per visit". Neither can happen if a call fills one slot. This is operator self-report. The system's per-visit log (due day 200 under R-P-029) is not on the charts, so it is unverified.
- **M3's path is blocked on this mechanism.** Seventy-two slots that need refills every few days cannot be kept selling on eight refills a day. The aim (≥60% by day 120) was missed and remains unchanged, and no plan shows how it could be reached. No redesign has lowered the goal or an aim, which is correct; the problem is that the path to M3 was designed on a wrong premise.
- **Value of the work:** the operator now runs one trip per slot and skips trips with little depot stock. Those are reasonable choices under the ceiling, not the cause of it.
- **Payouts under an open decision (human):** D-006 (refund policy) has been open since day 39/75. Under the standing policy the operator pays claims in full at the claimed amount, even where the item costs less: CMP-022 paid 19.20 against an item of at most 2.80 (day 199); CMP-017 paid 7.42 against 2.80 (day 150). This is a small cost leak, but it is a policy the human has not yet decided.

## Diagnosis

Pinpointed problem: the restock driver has a hard ceiling of one slot per 45-minute visit, which is about eight refills per day. Both redesigns that targeted it (R-P-020 and R-A-003) assumed visits could fill several slots. `slots_selling` has therefore not risen, and cash_gain_7 has shifted down since day 178. The gap to the exemplar has widened from 2.76 to 2.98 (gain basis) since day 150, and M3's path is blocked on this mechanism. The diagnostician should test: (a) the one-slot-per-call finding against the system's restock events, not the operator's self-report; (b) whether eight refills a day can keep 72 slots selling at the current sales rate, or whether the trip clock is the binding limit; (c) whether the cash_gain_7 shift from day 178 is explained by stock-outs or by a fall in revenue per slot, once the split exists.
