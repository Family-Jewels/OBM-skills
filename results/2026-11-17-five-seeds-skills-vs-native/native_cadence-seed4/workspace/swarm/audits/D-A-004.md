No changes to the business. No Bash access in this session, so all figures were computed by hand from the metrics table; they are rounded.

## What is going wrong

1. **Units sold have roughly halved, and the drop is in how many slots sell.** Selling-slot-days per day were 14.3 over days 128-134, 7.7 over days 135-141, and 7.5 over days 142-154. Units per day fell from 69 to 28-30. Revenue per unit did not move (2.26, 2.41, 2.21, 2.49 in the same windows), so this is volume, not price.

2. **Purchases now run at about 70% of revenue, against about 17% before day 135.** The cash bridge cannot be checked against order records, which do not exist. Taking the metrics identity (purchases = revenue − 12/day fee − change in cash):

| window | revenue | purchases (implied) | units | purchases per unit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| days 128-134 | 1,086 | 182 | 481 | 0.38 |
| days 135-141 | 421 | 355 | 175 | 2.03 |
| days 142-148 | 572 | 324 | 259 | 1.25 |
| days 149-154 | 316 | 227 | 127 | 1.79 |

   The "unexplained gap" that A-004 and A-003 flagged (about 355 over days 135-141) matches the implied purchases almost exactly. It is most likely stock bought, not a leak. The problem is that heavy buying from day 135 did not bring sales back.

## Why (ranked, with evidence)

1. **Most likely: stock bought from day 135 is not reaching priced, selling slots.** Purchases rose about fourfold per unit sold, and units sold fell at the same time. Slots fell from 16 (day 135) to 6 (day 136). Restocking that does not restore sales points to slots that are unpriced, or to stock in slots that do not sell. Swaps and refills may clear prices, which A-003 raised as a possibility. Not confirmed: no 72-slot census exists.
2. **Less likely: demand loss at current prices.** Revenue per unit is unchanged, and the count of selling slots fell rather than the sales per slot. A price-driven fall would show the opposite.
3. **Contributing only: the weekly dry pair** (days 7k and 7k+1). Days 140-141 and 147-148 were low, but the dry pattern does not explain the 135-154 fall.

## Ruled out
- **Solvency:** the balance (8,192 at day 154) covers about 680 days of the 12/day fee.
- **Per-unit pricing:** revenue per unit stayed in the 2.1-2.8 range throughout.
- **Settlement timing:** card lag moves single days (for example day 108, +21.60 with zero sales) but cancels over the 7-day windows above.

## What would settle it
- The 72-slot census for day 155 (empty, unpriced, priced-with-stock), with units per slot. If many restocked slots are unpriced, cause 1 is confirmed.
- The order record for days 135-154 (supplier, items, quantities, unit price). Purchases per unit of 2.0 against a sale price of about 2.3 would mean stock is bought near retail, or bought for slots that cannot sell.

Diagnosis needed: yes (slot-level cause unconfirmed; cash-side signature now identified).
