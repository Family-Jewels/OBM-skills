## 1. Is the direction right?
Yes, in part. Cash is the right top measure, and restocking and pricing are the right levers. But the tree cannot see the cause. Empty slots come from two sources that look the same on slots_selling: no stock in storage (supply) and five trips used with stock waiting (trips). The tree has no driver for either, so the operator cannot tell which one to fix. The trip cap is a budget the spec set on day 1, never tested. So the tree itself is missing drivers, and the design is the cause, not the goal or the aims.

## 2. Change (one change: make the limits visible and test the cap)
- Tree: add fills per day; sold-out selling slots (slot-days at zero stock); storage per sold-out slot; order-to-shelf lead time per supplier. Push now moves to fills and sold-out slot-days. Lead time is measured, not yet a checked criterion.
- Budget: a 6th fill is allowed as a test on days 110 to 125, only when a selling slot is sold out, storage holds its stock, and the day's reads and price changes still get done.
- Operator rules: log the reason for each selling slot still empty after the day's fills (rule 4); the test and fill count (rule 8); record order, due and landing days per order (rule 9).

Not changed, on purpose: check 3 (R-A-001 change kept, see section 8); incentives for stock-outs (the weakest cause, D-A-002 says so; deferred so this change can be measured alone); lead time as a checked criterion (deferred to the next redesign).

## 3. Why this change
It addresses the primary cause in D-A-002: missing information on supply versus trips. The test answers the one open question the diagnosis names, whether 5 fills a day is a real limit. It removes no check and gives the operator no new way to pass without sales. A 6th fill is still a sale only if stock is in storage, and check 4 and check 5 still apply.
