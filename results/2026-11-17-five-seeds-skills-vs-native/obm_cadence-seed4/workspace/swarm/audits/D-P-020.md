## What maintains it
- Stock-outs cost nothing visible: the sales report shows units sold, not units wanted, so a sold-out slot looks like low demand.
- Restock days bring a sales spike, which reads as success. The short-cover, small-order cycle is rewarded at each landing and never checked against the next sold-out day.
- Each sold-out day first looked like a trip problem, so the operator added trips; the supply gap then reappeared. The diagnosis alternated and was never settled because the test answer was not applied.

## Cause
1. **Information (primary):** the tree still has no chartable supply driver. Sold-out slot-days, storage per sold-out slot and effective lead time are not charted, so the operator cannot see that supply, not trips, binds. The spec's cover rule is not a criterion; cover is set by the operator and does not include the +1 day landing.
2. **Resources:** supplier lead time is 4 days quoted, 5 effective. The ≤3-day rule (P-015 pivot) leaves one supplier, so each stock line is exposed to a single delivery cycle. The €75 minimum forces small orders.
3. **Incentives (partial):** a stock-out has no cost on any check, so under-covering is not penalised. The records do not show the operator being pushed to hold more stock, so this is secondary.
4. **Not the cause:** the trip cap (tested; not binding), money (not binding), demand (Sunday and weekday multipliers explain the daily swings only).

## Design would need to change (not written here)
- Chart sold-out slot-days, storage per sold-out slot, fills and effective lead time (quoted plus landing delay) by day 150. Move push onto them.
- Give cover a measured criterion: stock in storage at least effective lead time plus 1 day of sell-through per selling line.
- Decide whether to apply the R-A-002 revert clause or to keep the storage and lead-time driver. The designer must record it.
- Add a stock-out cost that the sales report hides, so an empty slot costs something.
