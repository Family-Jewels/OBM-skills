# Redesign R-A-001 (day 81 of 365)

Trigger: Diagnosis D-A-001 (day 75), from full audit A-001. Charts at day 81 confirm the stall: cash 2,660 vs exemplar 12,056; slots_selling mean 8.6 since day 37; cash_gain_7 shift up on the old limits but still far below pace.

## 1. Is the direction right?
Yes. The top measure (final cash) and its drivers (slots_selling, revenue_per_slot, margin, cost of goods) are the right ones. Restocking is the lever, and the tree already says so. The fault is not the direction but the check that scores it: the weekly check counts stock bought as a loss, so holding cash passes and restocking looks like a fall. The design, not the goal or the aims, is the cause.

Not changed: the goal, the exemplar, the day-365 end date, the aims, and the checkpoint pace. The human's checkpoint reset (D-001) is a human decision and stays parked.

## 2. Change (spec v2 -> v3; spec.md updated in place, v2 kept at swarm/history/spec.v2.md)
Check 3 now reads **cash_gain_7 plus change in stock at cost**, with a 14-day rule: stock counts at unit cost only if bought or sold within the last 14 days; older unsold stock counts at zero. Check 2 (cash ≥ reserve) still reads cash alone. Check 4 still requires cash_gain_7 (cash only) to be positive. The final score is unchanged: cash only.

One change only. Adding drivers (fills, trips, storage, sold-out days per slot) and giving unused budget a consequence are the next redesign candidates, deferred so this change can be measured.

## 3. Why this change
It removes the scoring that rewards holding cash and penalises restocking, and it does so without adding a way to pass without sales: the 14-day rule means stock that does not sell earns nothing, and check 6 (sell-through after 14 days) still applies.

## 4. Prediction (testable on the charts)
- **slots_selling** shifts up: its 14-day mean is at least 11 by **day 100** (it has averaged 8.6 since day 37; the pre-redesign upper limit is 10.37).
- **revenue_last_7** rises above its same-7-days-a-month-earlier value (516.6) by **day 100**.
- **Stock at cost** rises first (days 82–95), then falls back as sales repay it. A rise that does not later fall means the change is being gamed, not used.

Checked by the auditor at the day-100 full audit, read against the charts (old limits kept).

## 5. Cost
- More cash tied up in stock (bounded by the existing weekly cap of 60% of free cash and the reserve).
- Operator must compute stock at cost from order receipts until the system charts it. This is an instrumentation gap, recorded in the spec.
- Risk: slow-selling stock held up to 14 days.

## 6. Revert if (by day 100)
- slots_selling 14-day mean is still ≤ 9.1 AND units sold have not risen, while stock at cost has risen for 14 days; or
- stock older than 14 days exceeds 25% of stock at cost on any day (parking, not selling); or
- any price is below cost to clear stock (check 5 still applies, so this is a breach, not a design fault).

On revert, restore spec v2 from swarm/history/spec.v2.md and record the reason in the day-100 audit.

## 7. Measurement faults noted (system or designer; not changed here)
- PIP on the charts uses exemplar gain ÷ swarm gain (9.1), not the spec's exemplar cash ÷ swarm cash. The spec is the reference. The system should chart the cash ratio at each pulse.
- The day-75 exemplar value (11,018) is an interpolation; the spec says so. The day-81 chart cites 12,056, a scripted reference value. Neither changes the spec.
- The main table's limits reset at day 37. The protocol says keep pre-redesign limits for redesign comparisons. The since-R-P-004 table is the one to read for the shift.
- Trips, fills, spend and operator reasons after day 50 are not in the workspace (log/, queue/). The day-100 audit needs them; the system should provide the log before then.
