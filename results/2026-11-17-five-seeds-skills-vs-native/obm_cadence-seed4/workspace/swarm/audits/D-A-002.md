## Pinpointed problem
From day 82 to day 102, after each day's trips 6 to 10 of 72 slots are still empty (log, days 98 to 102). Slots_selling stays near 10 and cash gains average ~235 a week against a check-3 pace that is already ~508. The machines have stock on hand for some of these slots and not for others, so the empty slots have two causes that alternate:
- **Supply-bound:** days 98 to 100, all three crisps slots and the energy slots were empty because storage held no crisps or energy until PO-1029 landed on day 101.
- **Trip-bound:** days 101 to 102, storage held 40 crisps and 30 energy while crisps slots stayed empty. Five fills a day were used.

## What maintains it
- **The 5-trip cap is a budget the spec set,** not a measured limit: "8 working hours … at most 5 trips per day, leaving about 2 hours for reads" (spec Budgets). The log calls it "the revenue ceiling" (day 67). No record tests a sixth fill. D-001 asked the designer this on day 30 and it is still unanswered.
- **Stock-outs are invisible.** The sales report shows units sold, not units wanted. A slot that sells out at 10 units records 10. The operator therefore sees no cost for empty slots, and the lost sales appear in no chart.
- **Late stock comes from lead time the tree does not track.** Order to landing, from runs.jsonl as logged: PO-1025 5 days, PO-1026 3, PO-1027 5, PO-1028 3, PO-1029 4, PO-1030 4. Two anomalies: PO-1031 planned 10 days, PO-1032 (Bakker) 12 days. The operator sizes each order to 7-day sell-through (A-001 action 1), so one late order empties slots for 1 to 3 days.
- **Cash is not the constraint.** Spend since day 82 is ~€100 to €250 an order against a cap above €1,000 a week; ~2,800 is free. The operator is not short of money.

## Cause
1. **Information (primary):** the tree has no driver for fills per day, storage per slot, sold-out slot-days or order-to-shelf lead time. The two causes above look the same on slots_selling, so the operator cannot tell which one to fix. Whether 5 trips is a real limit is untested.
2. **Resources:** the trip budget (self-set, untested) and supplier lead time (3 to 12 days, not controlled by the operator). These two cover the trip-bound and supply-bound days.
3. **Incentives (partial, not confirmed):** a stock-out costs nothing the operator is shown, and an unsold order at 14 days adds nothing to check 3. Under-ordering is therefore not penalised. I can't confirm this from the records; it is the weakest of the three.

Not a cause here: refunds (~€100 in total since day 59; small against the gap), the price tests (A-002 found no price-driven volume loss), the operator's arithmetic (the report-reality sample passed).

## Design would need to change (not written here)
- Add to the tree, and chart: fills per day with the reason for each day under 5; storage per slot; sold-out slot-days; order-to-shelf lead time per supplier. Move slots_selling onto them.
- Settle whether 5 fills a day is a real limit. The designer decides how to test it.
- Give stock-outs a visible cost: a lost-sales or unmet-demand measure, so an empty slot costs something.
- Make lead time a checked criterion for each order, so Bakker-style 12-day supply is not repeated.
- Fix the PIP definition and the pace path (the spec's geometric path is the designer's construct, not exemplar data).

## Evidence that would settle it
- runs.jsonl order, due and landing days per supplier, for all orders (the lead-time distribution).
- For each day from 60 on: fills done, and the logged reason for any day under 5.
- The 22 points behind the "since R-A-001" tests, to check the 8-in-a-row rule (the chart says "no shift" without showing them).
- Whether a sixth fill in a day is possible in the business tools (the one test that settles the trip cap).
