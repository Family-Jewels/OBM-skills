# Diagnosis D-A-001 (day 75 of 365), from full audit A-001

## Walk down the tree
- **Final cash (top):** 2,416 on day 75. Spec's own path (geometric, 3,228 on day 30 to 5,470 on day 90) gives ~4,800 on day 75. Swarm is ~2,400 short, about half the path. PIP by the spec's definition (exemplar cash ÷ swarm cash) = 11,018 ÷ 2,416 = **4.56**, not the 10.4 on the chart (see Measurement).
- **cash_gain_7 (weekly check):** 157.7 on day 75. Path pace is ~300 a week at this level. Below pace; check 3 fails.
- **revenue_last_7 (check 4):** 490.5 on day 75 against 516.6 a month earlier. Check 4 fails. Revenue has been flat at ~80/day since about day 37.
- **slots_selling (driver that moved):** 5 of 72 on day 75; 8–10 typical since day 37 (chart centre 9.1). This is the driver that did not grow while cash sat idle.
- **revenue_per_slot, units:** routine variation. Not the problem.

## Pinpointed problem
From day ~37 to day 75 the swarm keeps about 8 of 72 slots selling while its cash is 2,400 and its spend on stock stays far below its weekly cap (A-001: ~37% of cap, ~€1,640 idle on days 43–49). Empty slots stay empty; the cash does not become stock.

## What maintains it
- The weekly check counts stock as a loss on the day it is paid for; the sales that repay it come days later. Restocking therefore lowers the number the operator is judged on, while holding cash keeps cash_gain_7 positive and passes the floor. The short-cut pays at once; the correct path pays late. Open since D-P-004 and not changed by R-P-004.
- Nothing penalises idle cash. The weekly cap is a ceiling; no check or consequence reads use of budget.
- Trips are not always the limit (days 40, 45, 46, 49 used fewer than three trips; A-001 names missing stock on two of them). Trips explain some days, not all.

## Cause
1. **Incentives (primary):** the check's accounting makes restocking look like loss; idle cash passes.
2. **Information:** the tree has no driver for fills per day, storage held per slot, or sold-out days per slot, so the operator cannot see what keeps slots empty (A-001: more than one reading fits the log).
3. **Resources (partial):** the 5-trip cap and the 8-hour time budget bind on some days only.

## Measurement faults (system and designer, not agent behaviour)
- PIP uses the gain ratio (10.4), not the spec's cash ratio (4.56).
- The day-75 exemplar (11,018) is a straight-line interpolation; the spec says intermediate values are unknown.
- The main table resets limits at day 37; the protocol says keep the pre-redesign limits. The since-R-P-004 table reads "no shift yet" for cash_gain_7, whose 39-point mean (150) lies far above its pre-redesign centre (3.7). Under the protocol's run rule that looks like a shift. Check.
- Not in the workspace: log/ (progress, runs.jsonl, work.jsonl), queue/decisions.md, swarm/index.md. Trips, fills, spend and the operator's stated reasons after day 50 could not be read.

## Design would need to change (not written here)
- Show cash plus stock at cost in the weekly check, so restocking is not scored as loss. Move cash_gain_7 and check 3.
- Add drivers: fills per day, trips per day, storage per slot, sold-out days per slot, weekly spend against cap. Move slots_selling and units.
- Give unused budget a consequence (explain or deploy). Move capital deployed against free cash.
- Answer D-001 at the next redesign. The checkpoint reset is a human decision.

## Evidence that would settle it
Trips, fills and storage per slot for days 50–75, and the operator's logged reason for each empty slot. Without these, the trip-versus-supply question stays open.
