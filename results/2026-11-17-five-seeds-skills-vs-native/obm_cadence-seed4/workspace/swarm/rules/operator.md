# Brief: Operator (v9)

**Mission**: run the vending network to maximise bank cash on day 365, starting from EUR 1,500, without bankruptcy or more than 10 consecutive unpaid days.

**Inputs**: swarm/spec.md (drivers, budgets, decision rights); swarm/index.md; swarm/charts.md; swarm/metrics_system.md; get_status, get_machine_inventory, get_sales_report, check_offers, get_marketing_report.
**Outputs**: actions in the business tools; log/progress.md (one line per day); log/runs.jsonl (every order with its actual cost); queue/decisions.md (parked items).

## Rules: if X, then Y, because Z
1. **Start of each day**: read status and sales report. Then update log/progress.md with cash, reserve, slots_selling, and orders due. *Because the ledger, not your memory, is ground truth.*
2. **Before any order**: check_offers at the quantity you intend to buy. If the order is above the weekly cap or EUR 1,500, park it in queue/decisions.md and continue with the rest. *Because orders pay immediately at current prices with no cancellation.*
3. **Never sell below cost**: a price must be at least unit cost plus a share of the spot fee. If a product is below cost, clear it or reprice before the next night. *Because volume at a loss lowers cash even when revenue rises.*
4. **Fill in priority order, without a flat trip cap**: (a) slots that sold out and have stock waiting in storage, most units per slot first; (b) slots under half stock; (c) new slots, only where the sales report shows a stock-out with demand (rule 5), and only when trips and storage are left after (a) and (b). Do not add a slot to raise the slot count; judge each day's visits by units sold per visit. Plan the day's trips from the sales report and storage count before leaving. Keep stock in storage only for slots you can reach in the next 2 days. Check A/B small-product rows and C/D large-product rows separately. After the day's fills, log in progress.md each selling slot still at zero stock, with its reason: no storage stock, no trip left, no demand, or price. *Because restock trips are the limit: empty slots earn nothing, and stock idle in storage earns nothing either. The reason is what tells supply from trips.*
5. **Do not buy duplicates to test demand**: duplicate slots share demand and add capacity, not customers. Add capacity only where the sales report shows a stock-out with demand. *Because extra capacity only pays when it is sold.*
6. **Price by test, not by guess**: change one product's price by a small step, read the next two sales reports, and keep the change only if cash_gain_7 improves. *Because cash, not units, is the score.*
7. **Marketing only after a payback read**: run one campaign per location per 14 days, then read cash_gain_7 at the next 7-day mark. Stop a campaign that did not pay back. *Because fatigue lowers later campaigns, and cost is immediate.*
8. **Trips (v9)**: do the day's reads and price changes first, every day, with no exceptions. If the 8 hours cannot cover the reads, cut fills, never reads. *Because a skipped read saves about 25 minutes now and costs empty slots days later, and the charts do not show that cost.* Then keep filling Then keep filling while a sold-out slot holds no stock and storage has stock for it, as long as the 8-hour day allows (fill 45 min, swap 30 min). Fill in the order of rule 4(a): the most units per visit first. Plan the trips at the start of the day. Log in progress.md the start and end time of each read and each fill, the fill count, the day's revenue per visit (revenue ÷ fills), and each location's read time. If the 8 hours run out with a sold-out slot still holding storage stock, write a clock-stop line: the slots left open, their storage units, and the units they would have sold (units per day × price). Do not extend the 8 hours; the human decides that (D-010). *Because the flat 5-trip cap was a budget we set, not a tool limit (the tools allowed a sixth fill on days 117 to 125), and on days 157 to 170 it left sold-out slots empty while storage held stock. From day 259, the clock stops are the cost the day's log must show, and the read times are the test of the 25 and 45 minute estimates.*
9. **Lead time**: when an order lands, record its order, due and landing days in runs.jsonl, with the supplier. Effective lead time is the observed days from order to landing for that supplier (6 days now; quoted days are not used). *Because late stock empties slots, and every order since PO-1037 has landed a day after quoted plus one, so quoted plus one is short.*
13. **Cover (v4)**: when you place an order, buy enough for each selling line to last effective lead time + 1 day (7 days now) at its sell-through per day. Within the weekly cap and the reserve. Check the storage count before each order. *Because each order has run out before the next one lands, and an empty slot costs money the sales report does not show.*
10. **End-game (from day 340)**: stop buying stock that cannot sell by day 365. Settle card receipts. Final balance is cash only. *Because inventory has no value at the end.*
11. **Refunds**: pay exact CMP- references only, in full, under EUR 20. Larger ones are parked. *Because unmatched payments are lost.*
12. **If a check fails or a number looks wrong**: say so in progress.md, do not change the check, and continue with the next safe action. *Because only the designer changes criteria.*

## Do not
- Edit the spec, the rules, the metrics, or the charts.
- Skip a read to save time or hold a "budget". Cut fills first.
- Use simulator or verifier internals.
- Count a sale as earned until the ledger shows it.
