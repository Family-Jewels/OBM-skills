## Findings

### 1. Direction: wrong, and the goal's main measures are falling

- **slots_selling** peaked at 11 (day 11) and was 10 on day 15. It fell to 6 (day 19) and 4 (day 20). Capacity in use is 4 of 72 slots (5.6%).

- **Gap to exemplar** on the balance basis (exemplar ÷ balance) widened from 1.79 (day 15) to 1.94 (day 20). It is still below 2. The chart's PIP is undefined because it uses gain, which is negative. See finding 2.
- **Cash reconciles.** Revenue to day 19 is 813.20. Purchases total 700.20 (six orders). Spot fees are 240 (20 days × 12). 1,500 + 813.20 − 700.20 − 240 = 1,373, against 1,366.94 on the chart. The gap is about 6. No hidden cash leak is visible.

### 2. Does the tree add up, and are its drivers the ones that move the goal?

- **The first driver is a count, not an outcome.** slots_selling counts stocked, priced slots. The spec's own capacity note says duplicate slots add capacity, not customers, so a slot count can rise without revenue. In this data, slots and revenue moved together, so it works as a lead. The tree does not show which is binding: slot count or sales per slot.
- **Units per slot by location is missing.** The spec says to read it from get_sales_report by location each week. No such figure appears in the log. Without it, the tree cannot be checked at the level where it is supposed to act.
- **cash_gain_7 treats stock purchases as losses.** The 7-day window that ends day 20 includes the day-18 order of 145.58. The chart therefore falls when stock is bought, even though the stock is not a cost until sold. The tree has no stock-on-hand measure to separate working capital from loss.
- **The PIP definition conflicts.** spec.md defines PIP as exemplar ÷ balance. charts.md computes exemplar gain ÷ actual gain. The gain form cannot show a gap while the gain is negative. Two sources disagree on the one ratio the spec uses to judge the gap.
- **Predicted shifts did not appear.** P-002 and P-003 expected more selling slots from restocking and filling empty slots. Slots fell from 10 (day 15) to 4 (day 20). The restocks on days 16–18 partly offset the stock-outs, but not enough.

### 3. Capacity and ceilings

- **Money is not the constraint.** Free cash above the 300 reserve is about 1,067. The weekly cap is 85% of free cash, about 900. Actual orders were 65 to 146 each. Money is available but not deployed.
- **Purchases are sized to last week's sales.** The day-8 log says sales are too thin to justify more stock. P-002 pivot 1 held purchases outside three products. Together these have made last week's sales the limit on this week's stock. This is a design effect, not a rule breach, but it means the target "slots at full stock" (M3) has become a ceiling on slots_selling.
- **Lead time is set against a weekly rhythm.** Bakker takes 7 to 9 days from order to delivery. Depot stock ran out on days 8, 12 and 16, and empty slots waited for the next delivery. The next delivery (Bakker, due day 20) and the next Zuidas order (due day 22) are both after the slots fell.
- **About 250 units are not accounted for.** Units bought to day 19 total 648. Units sold (system rows) total 398. Known expiry is 8 sandwiches. That leaves about 240 to 250 units (about 270 at average cost of 1.08). The records do not show where they are. They may be in unpriced slots, the depot, or lost. Only get_machine_inventory can settle it. Unverified.

## Diagnosis

The pinpointed problem is that slots_selling and revenue fall when depot stock runs out. The weekly rhythm buys after the stock has gone, and the 7 to 9 day lead time turns each gap into a week or more of empty slots. Purchases are also sized to last week's sales, not to the cap. On top of this, about 250 units cannot be located in the records, and the designer must fix two measure definitions (the PIP form and the lack of a stock measure) and decide whether the slot count or the sales per slot is the lever.
