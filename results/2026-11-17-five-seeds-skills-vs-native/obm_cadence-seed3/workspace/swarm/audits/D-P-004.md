Trigger: P-004 `Diagnosis needed: yes`, on PIP 17.0 (gain basis) with no signalled improvement.

**Trigger check.** No chart point is beyond a limit, and no 8-run exists on any of the five measures (charts.md, day 26). The 17.0 figure is a definition conflict, not a signal: the spec defines PIP as exemplar cash ÷ swarm cash (2,997.6 ÷ 1,641.85 = 1.83 at day 26), while the chart uses gain ÷ gain (10.6). P-004 also says the log ends at day 20; it runs to day 25. The problems below are real, but they are found in the records, not on the charts.

**Problem (pinpointed).** Days 21–25: the depot ran dry while orders were in transit, so energy A1 (all 3 machines), all 6 crisps slots and most choco/nut/stroopwafel slots were empty. Separately, five complaints (€75.11) were paid at 4–13× item price, four of them for expired stock.

**Evidence.** Log days 18–22: zuidas quoted about 2 days, delivered in 2–11 days (PO-1004 10, PO-1010 11); bakker 10–11 days. Orders were sized and timed to offer validity, and the 2-day assumption held until day 18. Day 22 log: "the depot ran dry while orders were in transit." Complaints CMP-001 to 005 all cite stock loaded days 4–10; no complaint after day 23 in the log.

**What maintains it.**
- Ordering on offer validity and a 2-day assumption; the late lead time is seen only on arrival, so each stock-out is discovered after it happens.
- Refunds: paying the full claim closes the complaint at once; paying the item price leaves it open. The operator follows C6 ("full amount") and its own rule. The cost is paid immediately and the complaint arrives later.
- Restocking to 10 units per slot at 2–3 sales a day gives about 4 days of cover per trip, so stock-outs recur between trips.

**Cause.**
1. **Information (main):** lead time is not in the spec or `rules/operator.md`, and is learned only after ordering. In-stock fraction and 7-day COGS are still not measured (spec open question 1, unmet at day 26), so the operator uses a manual empty-slot proxy. slots_selling (5 on day 22) does not reconcile with the log's ~30 non-empty slots. No shelf-life data is held, so C7's 21-day rule does not catch expiry.
2. **Incentives:** C6 "full amount" makes paying inflated claims the cheapest way to close. Nothing pays for item-price-first.
3. **Resources:** 8 trips a day at 45 min, 10-unit slots, and one main supplier until day 22 limit how many slots can be kept stocked at this demand.

**What the design would need to change (no change written here).**
- Define PIP once (cash ratio) and state the pulse trigger in that unit. Measure: PIP.
- Add lead time (realised, per supplier) to the brief, and order to cover the longest observed lead time. Measure: slots_selling, in-stock fraction.
- Instrument in-stock slot-days and 7-day COGS before the next redesign. Measure: in-stock fraction.
- Revise C6 so refunds use the item price unless evidence supports more; add shelf-life to records. Measure: refunds_7.
- Reconcile slots_selling with the system's definition and the log count.

**Would settle it.** Slot-days out of stock per day from the system; lead-time receipts by supplier; whether complaints recur on stock loaded after day 19.
