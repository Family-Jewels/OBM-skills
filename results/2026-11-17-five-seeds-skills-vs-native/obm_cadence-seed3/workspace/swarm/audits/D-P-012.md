**Problem (pinpointed).** From day 76 to 81 the stocked, selling slots fell and revenue_last_7 fell 32%. Revenue ÷ same week a month earlier was below 1 on all seven readings (0.66 to 0.84, day 75–81), so C4 has failed for a week. Part of this is a base effect: the comparison week is the day-45 peak.

**Evidence.**
- Charts day 81: revenue_per_slot 17.0, one point above its upper limit (16.94). Slots_selling 8 is inside limits but has been below centre 13.0 since day 71.
- Log: last entry is day 68, not day 47 as P-012 states. Days 69–81 are unrecorded.

**What maintains it.**
- Stock-outs recur because a restock gives 10 units per slot, which high-demand slots sell in about two days, while trips cap at 8 a day.
- C6 pays the full claim, so the cheapest way to close a complaint is to pay it. Expiry claims continue (CMP-008, day 66: €1.50 item, €17.64 balance). Refunds total €130 by day 66.
- The day-40 hold decision on mint and gummy (1 and 0 units that week) was reversed by days 58–64 sales (mint 28, gummy 35). The hold rule was set on one week of data.

**Cause.**
1. **Information (main).** In-stock fraction and 7-day COGS are still not measured at day 81 (spec open question 1, open since day 9). The operator counts empty slots by hand. PIP has two bases: the spec's cash ratio (2.91 at day 81) and the chart's gain ratio (4.0). The P-012 figure of 3.5 at day 75 does not match either basis from the charts. Lead time is 11–12 days, and cover targets were missed on several orders (days 38, 40, 45).
2. **Resources.** Eight trips a day at 45 minutes each. From day 47 machine reads were cut to one per restock day to save spend, which reduces the visibility that would show stock-outs. Spend is unreconciled: the spec says about USD 2 remains, the log says about USD 36 at day 47. `log/runs.jsonl` and `log/work.jsonl` do not exist, so spend and actions cannot be checked.
3. **Incentives.** C6 rewards paying inflated claims. Expiry has no shelf-life record, so C7 cannot run as written.

**What the design would need to change (not written here).**
- Require a complete daily record, checked at each pulse. Measure: records gap in days.
- Instrument in-stock slot-days and 7-day COGS before the next redesign. Measure: in-stock fraction.
- Make the reads-per-day cap and run budget consistent, so slot visibility holds when stock is short. Measure: slots_selling.
- One PIP basis, in the spec and the chart. Measure: PIP.
- Revisit C6 now and add shelf-life data. Measure: refunds_7.
- Test the 16-day cover target against order size, especially crisps. Measure: revenue_last_7.

**Corrections to P-012 the designer needs.** The log ends day 68, not 47. The "no chart shift on all five" claim was true at day 75 only. revenue_per_slot is now beyond its limit (day 81).

**Would settle it.** Slot-level stock-out counts and machine reads for days 69–81; the system's in-stock fraction; order receipts for those days; the run-spend ledger for the budget figure; and get_sales_report demand per slot, to split stock-outs from lower demand.
