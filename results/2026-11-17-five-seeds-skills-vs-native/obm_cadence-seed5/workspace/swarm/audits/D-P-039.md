## Pinpointed problem

Since day 287, units per selling slot is down about 20%: slots are stocked but sell less, because the depot holds no sellable stock on the mornings the machines need it. Observed in the records: PO-1069 (due 282) not in depot at 09:25 and no fills that day; PO-1076 (due 300) not in depot at 09:25, depot holding crisps only, nuts/gummy/cola/energy at zero, drinks and nuts/gummy slots empty; PO-1078 (energy 60, due 307) not in depot at 09:25, depot nuts 2 only.

## What maintains it

- **Late arrivals are normal, not rare.** Each late delivery lands the day after its due date. The cover count treats in-transit units as stock, so a late order keeps reading as cover while the slots run dry.
- **Fills are capped at about 6–9 trips a day.** Even when depot stock exists, slots reload late (D-P-021).
- **The top row still counts stock purchases as cost** (spec row 13), so a larger, earlier order looks like a loss for 7 days. This favours small or late orders.

## Cause

- **Information:** the 8-day cover floor counts in-transit units at their due date. It cannot see that a delivery has slipped. In-transit stock is logged as cover although it has not arrived. Lead time 3–4 days plus 1-day overrun is not in the trigger.
- **Resources:** depot supply timing (orders land late, depot empty on arrival days) and fill capacity (6–9 trips for about 30 slots).
- **Incentives:** the top row penalises buying (see above), so the signal favours small or late orders.
- **Knowledge / model tier:** not the cause. The operator logs the empty depot and the late deliveries itself.

## What the design would need to change (not written here)

- Count in-transit units as cover only up to their due date plus expected overrun, or chart cover on depot and machine stock alone.
- Chart stock-out slot-days and units per selling slot with their own limits in `metrics_system.md`, so the 09:00 count is not the only evidence.
- Split cash_gain_7 so stock purchases are not counted as losses (spec row 13).
