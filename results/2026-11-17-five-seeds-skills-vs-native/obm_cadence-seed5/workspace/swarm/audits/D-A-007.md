# Diagnosis D-A-007 (day 357 of 365)

Trigger: A-007 (`Diagnosis needed: yes`), with the chart signal `slots_selling` beyond its lower limit (WORSE, latest 0).

## Walk down the tree

- **Final balance:** 13,304.00 at day 357 (`metrics_system.md`). Exemplar 59,834 on the chart; cash-basis PIP 4.5 (chart's gain-basis 4.9). Gain has slowed: about 44/day from day 287 to 321, about 32/day from 321 to 357.
- **cash_gain_7 / revenue_last_7:** 630 at day 321, 339 at day 357 (falling since about day 330).
- **revenue = units × price; units = slots_selling × units per slot.** `slots_selling` is the driver that moved: 0 on rows 356 and 357, and beyond the lower limit on the chart. Units per slot fell from 5.6 to 4.5 after day 287 (A-007).
- **Mechanism:** priced slots empty at the morning count. 27 of 36 empty on day 330 (orchestrator, `queue/decisions.md`). Depot holds no sellable stock on fill mornings (days 330, 340, 355, 356).

## Pinpointed problem

On the morning each delivery is due, and for 1 to 4 days after, sellable stock is absent at the depot and the machines run empty. Every checkable order from PO-1075 to PO-1083 landed one day after due. PO-1088 (due 330) was not in the depot at 11:55. PO-1097 (due 356) was not in the depot at 09:25 on 356.

## What maintains it

- The cover trigger (8 days, 09:00, per location) assumes a 3 to 5 day lead. Observed lead is 4 to 6 days and every delivery is a day late. An order placed at the trigger therefore lands after the stock is gone. The cycle repeats each order.
- The gap costs revenue at once and is seen only at the next pulse (lapses: energy below 8 from day 327 ordered 327; crisps below 8 from day 326 ordered 330). Nothing makes a same-morning order more immediate than the next pulse.
- Late in the run the operator stopped ordering after about day 355 (sizing to "remaining days"). Spec M6 says keep machines full through day 365. Sales are 0 on rows 356 and 357.

## Cause

- **Information (primary):** the lead-time and 8-day floor the design relies on do not match observed arrivals. A-007 flagged this; the floor was deferred on three pulses and not changed in R-P-039. The spec also gives no end-game replenishment rule, so the operator wrote one that conflicts with M6.
- **Incentives:** a missed same-morning order shows up only at pulses, five days later.
- **Resources (records):** time log stops at day 294; zero counts for day 295 and days 318 to 329 are not all recorded; progress log has no entry after day 356. These block the revert test and this diagnosis.
- **Not supported:** the operator's effort or fill capacity. Cash (13,304) is far above the EUR 168 reserve, so cash was not the limit.

## R-P-039 status

Its prediction (priced slots empty at or below 10 of 30 on every count, days 318 to 330) is already failed by the one count on record (27 of 36, day 330). Its revert clause needs three counts above 15 in days 318 to 330; only one is recorded. Revert is undecided.

## What the design would need to change

- Set the lead time and cover floor from observed arrivals (including the one-day lag), and measure stock-out slot-days by day (the charted `slots_selling` is the outcome; stock-out slot-days is the lead measure).
- Add an explicit end-game replenishment rule that meets M6.
