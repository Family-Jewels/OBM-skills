## Pinpointed problem

Priced slots are empty for most of each stock cycle. On day 134 the operator counted 20 of 30 priced slots at zero (09:25–11:30; snacks 12 of 20, drinks 8 of 10). The depot was at 0–13 units per product from day 127 to 133, and the order placed on day 133 (PO-1030, EUR 582.60) arrives day 137. Gaps recur every 4–7 days (empty days 113–114, 119, 124–125, 133–137). The operator's count is not system ground truth; the system still has no stock-out measure.

## What maintains it

- **Calendar orders.** Orders are still placed on the offer's validity date (days 97, 104, 111, 133). The cover floor fired on days 117, 123 and 127, but each time after the gap had opened.
- **Rule 11 read as a weekly rule.** On days 129–132 depot cover for choco, crisps and dropjes was below 6 days (days 131: choco about 2, crisps 10, dropjes 4). Rule 11 says order that location's product that day, even if it ordered this week. The operator waited for day 133. PO-1029 had covered only sparkling, cola and energy.
- **Cost visible, lost sales invisible.** The order that prevents a gap is booked against cash on the day it is paid. The empty slots cost sales that no chart shows. The operator is rewarded for waiting and punished for ordering in time.
- **Refill rushes look like recovery.** Each restock week pays back fast (e.g. days 122–123, 141 units), which reinforces the loop.

## Cause

- **Order timing (main cause today):** the cover measure uses depot stock against a network 7-day rate and ignores machine stock and in-transit stock. A 6-day threshold against a 3–5 day lead, with one order per location per week, leaves a 1–4 day gap by design. On day 133 depot cover was 0.2 days for choco and 0 for energy and crisps when the order was placed.
- **Fill throughput (co-cause):** 6–9 trips a day against 22–30 slots. Snack slots of 18 units sell out in about 2 days (day 110). Even with stock in the depot (day 116: 14 choco, 18 dropjes), slots stay empty while trips run out.
- **Information:** rule 11 does not say whether cover is per location or network, or whether machine and in-transit stock count (A-003 F15). The stock-out driver marked "push now" has no chart or system column. The chart's exemplar and PIP series still disagree with the spec (A-003 F2).
- **Incentives:** cash_gain_7 counts purchases as losses; reserve and 15% rules make large early orders look costly; complaints paid in full on day 60 are a separate cash cost in the same signal.
- **Knowledge / model tier:** not the cause. The operator names the right fix in its log on days 110, 114, 124 and 131 each time.

## Evidence that would settle the split

Daily, for 14 days: depot units per product, machine slots at zero, trips made, and the day each order was placed against its cover. Days 116 and 131 already suggest throughput (depot stock present, slots empty); days 124–125 and 133–134 suggest timing (depot empty). A system stock-out column would replace the operator's count.

## What the design would need to change (not written here)

- Measure stock-out slot-days in the system with its own limits (the operator's count is not ground truth).
- Make cover count machine and in-transit stock against lead time, and state per-location or network explicitly in rule 11.
- Split cash_gain_7 into purchases, cost of goods sold and receipts in transit.
- Match fill capacity to slot count (time log, daily), or cut slots that sell through fastest.
- Settle one PIP and one exemplar series.
