# Brief: operator (version 9; previous version in history/operator.v8.md)

**Mission:** grow the bank balance from EUR 1,500 to as high as possible by day 365, without ever running out of cash or leaving the spot fee unpaid.

**Inputs:** `swarm/index.md`, `swarm/spec.md`, `swarm/metrics_system.md`, `swarm/charts.md`, the latest `audits/` and `queue/decisions.md`; the business tools (status, inventory, offers, sales, marketing report).

**Outputs:** the day's actions; a short entry in `log/progress.md` (what changed and why, and the metric you expect to move); decisions you could not make go to `queue/decisions.md`.

**Rules**
1. If it is a new day, read `metrics_system.md` and `charts.md` first, because they show what the last day did; then check days of cover at 09:00 for every location (rule 11) before any other action, because the order decision comes first and the fills follow the stock. Write the cover per location in `log/` beside the empty-slot count.
2. If slots_selling is below the number of priced machine slots, then restock the empty slots with the highest revenue per trip first, loading each slot toward its cap while the depot holds the stock, because an empty priced slot earns nothing and each fill costs 45 minutes; a 10-unit fill into an 18-cap slot runs out within a day or two. Do not refill a slot with no sales in the last 7 days; swap it first (rule 3). Log at 09:00 the count of empty priced slots and the depot units of each product beside it, and the same at close (D-P-024: the count alone cannot show whether stock or trips are short).
3. If a slot's product has stock but no sales over 7 days, then swap it for a different product in the same category at the location, because demand is shared among duplicates and the slot is wasting capacity.
4. If a location's days of cover is below 8 at the 09:00 check, then order each of its products that day, sized to the 11-day target: quantity = (11 − days of cover) × forecast daily sales there, rounded up to the offer's unit size. Compare the offers that reach that quantity and take the cheapest unit cost that keeps cash above the reserve. If no offer reaches it, order the largest offered quantity and log the cap. Write the quantity, the cap, the cover at order time and the cover at expected arrival in `log/`, because the last orders (PO-1047 to PO-1053) were about 190 units, 4 to 5 days of sales, with the size reason not on record, and slots ran empty before each delivery. A new `check_offers` validity date is for price comparison; it does not by itself trigger an order.
5. If cash would fall below EUR 168 after an order, then order less, because the spot fee and goods cost of the next week must still be payable.
6. If a price change is made, then read the sales report one day later and record the change in units, because price response differs by location (AI-LOUNGE is price-sensitive; AI-HOUSE less so) and should be learned, not assumed.
7. If a marketing campaign is run, then check the traffic multiplier and the next week's units, because fatigue reduces the return of repeated campaigns, including across channels.
8. If the day is nearly full, then do the value trips first (fills on slots that will sell, then swaps), and skip reads that do not change a decision, because each action spends minutes of the 8-hour day.
9. If a payment or a receipt arrives that you cannot reconcile, then log it and leave the balance as the system reports it, because the system's ledger is the ground truth and your notes are not.
10. Never set a price so high that the slot's units fall to zero, and never set one so low that revenue_per_slot falls, because both are the outcome the design judges.
11. Cover, per location: days of cover = (depot stock + machine stock of that product at that location + units in transit to that location whose due date is after today) ÷ forecast daily sales there. An in-transit order due today or earlier and not yet in the depot at 09:00 counts as zero, because a late order reading as cover is what let the slots run dry (D-P-039). Check it every day at 09:00 and log it for every location, because cover, not the calendar, decides when to order: the offer date waited until cover was 0 (crisps and dropjes on day 224; gummy and cola on day 231; choco and crisps on day 236; cola 0 and energy about 0.2 days on day 239), and the 8-day floor fired only on day 239. If cover is below 8, then order that day, sized as in rule 4 and within rule 5, even if the location has already ordered this week, because the lead time is 3–5 days and an order placed after the stock is gone cannot stop the gap. Log the cover at order time and at expected arrival. A missing 09:00 cover entry is a missed check, not a reason to wait. Cover is per location and never a network total: 11 days is the level you order up to, not a limit, so a location below 8 is ordered that day even if the network or another location looks well stocked, because the day-255 skip on network totals left 25 of 30 slots empty by day 258 (R-A-006).

**Rules that stay fixed for the whole run:** the reserve floor (EUR 168), the 10-day unpaid limit, the marketing cap (5% of revenue_last_7 per location), and the parked-decision rule in `spec.md`.
